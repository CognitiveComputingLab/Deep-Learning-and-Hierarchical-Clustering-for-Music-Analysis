# Related work: positioning for the paper

This is a source-checked draft of the comparison argument. The method here constructs **ordered, contiguous binary trees over symbolic pitch-class bins**. It separates a similarity representation from a tree search procedure. That combination is the useful unit of comparison; neither the use of a neural encoder nor the use of dynamic programming is, by itself, a novelty claim.

## Similarity-based hierarchical clustering without temporal order

[Dasgupta (STOC 2016)](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~dasgupta/papers/hier-cost.pdf) gives a cost for a hierarchy using pairwise similarities, making a complete tree an object that can be scored independently of the algorithm that constructed it. [Moseley and Wang (NeurIPS 2017)](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/hash/d8d31bd778da8bdd536187c36e48892b-Abstract.html) study a revenue-style dual and approximation properties of familiar clustering procedures. Both motivate an explicit whole-tree objective and the distinction between heuristic search and objective value. Their general clustering problems allow groupings that need not be contiguous in time. Our interval constraint makes exact dynamic programming possible for the **specified additive objective**, but their approximation guarantees do not establish agreement with expert musical analyses or transfer automatically to the added boundary and balance terms.

## Grammar-based musical parsing and dynamic programming

[Granroth-Wilding and Steedman, *A Robust Parser-Interpreter for Jazz Chord Sequences* (2014)](https://mark.granroth-wilding.co.uk/files/jnmr_accepted.pdf) map chord sequences to structured harmonic interpretations using a formal grammar and statistical parsing. [Harasim et al., *The Jazz Harmony Treebank* (ISMIR 2020)](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2020/paper/000080.pdf) supply full chord-sequence syntax trees and a formalism of harmonic prolongation and preparation. These works provide a relevant musical interpretation of hierarchy and a stronger supervised syntax target than a flat boundary list. Their leaves are chords and their internal relationships carry harmonic functions. Our interval DP has a similar split recurrence, but its states contain only temporal intervals and scores; it has no nonterminal symbols, production rules, or harmonic interpretation labels. Consequently, this paper should say **exact ordered-tree inference**, not “grammar parsing.”

## Musical structure and hierarchical evaluation

[McFee and Ellis, *Analyzing Song Structure with Spectral Clustering* (ISMIR 2014)](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2014/2014_ISMIR_Proceedings.pdf) derive multiscale repetition structure from audio self-similarity. It is relevant to the representation-versus-structure question, while its audio features, repetition focus, and graph method differ from this symbolic-score pipeline. [McFee, Nieto, and Bello, *Hierarchical Evaluation of Segment Boundary Detection* (ISMIR 2015)](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2015/paper/000174.pdf) define T-measures from rankings of structural proximity, avoiding a forced correspondence between reference and predicted depths. We use their hierarchy-aware principle for Taking Form, with the implementation choices specified in [DATA_AND_EVALUATION.md](DATA_AND_EVALUATION.md).

[Gotham and Ireland, *Taking Form* (ISMIR 2019)](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2019/paper/000084.pdf) introduce representations and a corpus of expert form analyses, including nested annotations. Their [corpus documentation](https://github.com/MarkGotham/Taking-Form) specifies coarse-to-fine levels, measure/beat positions, and repeat notation. These annotations offer a genuine form-hierarchy target, subject to score alignment and partial depth. By comparison, the [Annotated Beethoven Corpus](https://github.com/DCMLab/ABC) supplies notes and expert harmonic annotations; its local-key changes support a tonal-boundary task but do not supply an expert form tree.

## Claim that this literature supports testing

The candidate contribution is an empirical study of how **representation, ordered-tree objective, inference algorithm, and annotation target interact**. Exact inference establishes the best value of its own fixed objective. Whether that tree agrees more closely with expert form annotations must be measured separately. The current ABC and Taking Form experiments give complementary evidence, with different ground truth and some different objective specifications; the paper must preserve those distinctions rather than pooling scores.
